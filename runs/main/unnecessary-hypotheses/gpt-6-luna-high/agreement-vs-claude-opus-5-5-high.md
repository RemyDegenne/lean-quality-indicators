Let A be the path component of p in U and A' the path component of p in V. Since U ∩ V is path-connected (hI) and contains p, we have U ∩ V ⊆ A ∩ A', and therefore A ∩ A' = U ∩ V.

(1) The path component PC of p in X lies in A ∪ A'. Take a path γ from p. By hcover and a Lebesgue-number argument, γ splits into finitely many pieces, each lying in U or in V. Every junction between a U-piece and a V-piece lies in U ∩ V ⊆ A ∩ A'. By induction, each U-piece starts in A and so stays in A, and each V-piece stays in A'.

(2) From (1), U ∩ PC = A: a point of U ∩ PC lies in A or in A' ⊆ V, and in the second case it lies in U ∩ V ⊆ A. Likewise V ∩ PC = A'. So A and A' are open in PC and cover it, both are path-connected, and their intersection U ∩ V is path-connected.

(3) Every loop at p, and every homotopy of such loops, has path-connected image containing p. So the inclusions A ↪ U, A' ↪ V and PC ↪ X induce isomorphisms of fundamental groups at p.

Now apply the theorem itself, with all its hypotheses, to the space PC and the cover A, A'. Pull φU and φV back along the isomorphisms π₁(A,p) ≅ π₁(U,p) and π₁(A',p) ≅ π₁(V,p). The compatibility condition carries over, because the map U ∩ V → U factors through A. The theorem then gives a unique φ' : π₁(PC,p) →* G. Composing φ' with the inverse of π₁(PC,p) ≅ π₁(X,p) gives the required φ. Existence and uniqueness follow because the isomorphisms in (3) are bijective and every square commutes.

The degenerate cases are covered: `p` makes U ∩ V nonempty. Hence hU can be dropped. The same argument applies to hV, and in fact both can be dropped together.
- `(hV : IsPathConnected (V : Set X))`: This is symmetric to hU. Let A' be the path component of p in V (and A that of p in U). hI and `p ∈ U ∩ V` give U ∩ V ⊆ A ∩ A'. Splitting a path into pieces by a Lebesgue-number argument, using hcover, shows that the path component PC of p in X lies in A ∪ A'. It follows that V ∩ PC = A' and U ∩ PC = A, so A and A' are an open cover of PC by path-connected sets with path-connected intersection U ∩ V. The inclusions A ↪ U, A' ↪ V and PC ↪ X induce isomorphisms on π₁ at p, because loops and homotopies at p stay in the path component of p. So the universal property for X, U, V follows from the theorem (with all its hypotheses) applied to PC, A, A', by transporting along these isomorphisms. Thus hV can be deleted; hU and hV can even be deleted simultaneously.
